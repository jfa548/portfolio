Four sharp ad concepts that make "live data in your spreadsheet" obvious in a single frame, each built for feed, story and display.

The brief

Coefficient keeps spreadsheets connected to live company data. That's a useful but unglamorous promise, and paid social gives it about a second to land. One message wasn't going to cut through, so the campaign needed several bold directions, each dramatising a different reason the product matters, and each working at every placement.

What I did

Developed four campaign concepts: Close the Gap, The Infrastructure Layer, Describe It. Get It., and RevOps as Leverage. Designed each concept across landscape, square feed and vertical story formats, plus display banners. Built a shared ad system (type, colour, layout logic) so four different ideas still read as one brand.

Key decisions

1. Show the gap, don't explain it.
The lead concept puts a torn "Monday export" next to live dashboards, so stale vs. real-time numbers are obvious at a glance. In a scrolling feed, the image has to do the work before anyone reads the copy.

2. Four angles instead of one message.
Different buyers care about different things: accuracy, category ambition, AI speed, RevOps leverage. Separate concepts let each angle hit its audience, and give the team more creative to test against each other.

3. Cut for the placement, not resized.
Each concept is recomposed for feed, story and display rather than scaled down. That keeps it recognisable whether it's a square post or a full-screen story.

01 — Close the Gap

The lead idea makes the value visible in a single image: a torn “Monday export” on one side, live dashboards on the other, and the gap between stale and real-time numbers impossible to miss. The tear does the talking before a word is read.

Square feed format. Vertical story format. Note: Dashboard figures shown are illustrative.

02 — The infrastructure layer

“1.1 billion people open Excel every day. Nobody is building the infrastructure layer — nobody except one company.” A giant gradient wordmark reframes a familiar tool as an untapped platform, and makes a category claim with total confidence.

The “infrastructure layer” direction, in landscape.

03 — Describe it. Get it.

“Describe the dashboard. Get the Dashboard.” A plain-language prompt resolves into a live pipeline report, pulled from HubSpot with NRR and quota attainment — the product’s promise shown as a before-and-after in one frame.

The AI-prompt direction — request in, live dashboard out.

04 — RevOps as leverage

“The RevOps function your CRO is jealous of.” An org chart reframes the spreadsheet as leverage rather than grunt work — a sharper, insider angle aimed squarely at revenue teams.

The RevOps leverage direction, in landscape.

Across every format

Each concept is cut for the placement it runs in — feed, story and display — so the campaign stays recognisable whether it’s a square post or a full-screen story.

Infrastructure — story. Describe it — story. RevOps — story.

Result

A flexible, multi-concept ad system: four distinct ideas that each stand on their own, united by one voice and built to run cleanly across every paid-social and display placement.
